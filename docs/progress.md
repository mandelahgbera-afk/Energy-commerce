## Where things stand

**Built and tested, on a laptop only:** the money and stock database in `supabase/migrations/`. It covers:

- staff roles, customers and suppliers
- double-entry ledger, month close and reversals
- stock with serial numbers, holds, blind counts and exact stock value
- orders with separate money and goods tracks: deposits, part payment, part delivery, credit, discount approvals, VAT
- transfers confirmed against bank lines, tills, refunds and opening balances
- row-level security on every table, and role checks on every function

- returns and credit notes, owner money and blind safe counts, card taps waiting for the bank, quotes, part search
- the counter's price preview and the owner's day (`owner_today`)

**Tests:** 126 rule scenes on PGlite and real Postgres, 19 race scenes, all recorded in `sim/baseline.json` with every migration locked (raised 2 Nov after an outside review). Outside reviews have now found 15 serious bugs in the first core and 3 in the 2 Nov work. All are fixed, with scenes.

**Screens (Next.js):** `/counter` (sale and till), `/owner` (the owner's day), `/kit`, `/login`. They build and type-check, and their maths has helper tests, but **no screen has yet run end to end with a signed-in user**. The counter's first version had four money bugs that only that would have caught.

**Staging:** the owner's free Supabase project has all 13 migrations, recorded in `ops.applied_migrations`. Apply new ones with `npm run db:apply` (a dry run), then `--yes`. Nothing is deployed for customers. The shop visit (plan Phase 0) hasn't happened and is still required before go-live.

Retry-safe, counter and accountant only. Proof: 101 rule scenes (92 old + 9 new) on PGlite and real Postgres, 15 race scenes (13 old + 2 new). - [x] **Owner's money and the safe.** `owner_money_in` / `owner_money_out` (owner only since the 2 Nov review): the owner puts money in or takes it out against `owner_money` (equity), never a running cost, never through a till, reversible. Blind safe counts: a counter counts (since the 2 Nov review, not the accountant, who can see the expected figure), the owner decides, and the difference lands in a new `safe_differences` account; a stale count (money moved or a till opened since it was taken, judged from the caller's counted-at time) is refused, and two old counts can't take the same loss twice. `cash_position()` shows ledger cash = tills + safe. Close-month waits for an undecided safe count. Proof: 110 rule scenes (101 old + 9 new) on PGlite and real Postgres, 17 race scenes (15 old + 2 new). A second reader found 4 holes (a payment racing the approval created cash, staleness measured from tap-in time, a submit slipping past month close, tills opening mid-approval) — all fixed with locks and counted-at, with scenes. Open: whether a monthly safe count is mandatory; a wrong count is fixed by recounting, not reversal (same as stock and till counts).
- [x] **POS settlement.** A card tap at the counter now hands over the goods but stays pending like a transfer, until a second person matches it against a bank line: goods still leave, the counter is not slowed, and a fake tap stays a customer debt that shows on the owner's list, never money. Cash on top of a waiting card tap is refused, so a customer can't be charged twice; delivery counts waiting taps toward the covered amount. Proof: 114 rule scenes (110 old + 4 new) on PGlite and real Postgres, 18 race scenes (17 old + 1 new). A second reader found 1 hole (cash double-charge) and a millisecond false-staleness on real Postgres — both fixed with scenes. Open: after a rejected tap, the unpaid delivered order shows in debtors, like a bounced transfer.
- [x] **Quotes.** A quote is a promise on paper: no money moves and no stock is held until accepted. Each version is its own record with its own Q number; only the latest unexpired version can be accepted, and acceptance becomes an ordinary order at the quoted price — even after prices change — with the discount rules judged against the current list, so a big drop waits for the owner like any other. Trade prices and VAT rounding match orders exactly. Proof: 118 rule scenes (114 old + 4 new) on PGlite and real Postgres, 19 race scenes (18 old + 1 new). A second reader's ten attacks found no break. Open: the discount limit judged at acceptance is the accepter's; approval at quote time is a shop decision.
- [x] **Catalogue search and substitutes.** `search_parts` finds a part however it is asked: "6205 2RS", "6205-2rs" and "62052RS" all hit the same row, 25 × 52 × 15 finds everything that fits, a prefix or a typo still lands, and "same part" is told apart from "fits but differs". `pg_trgm` became a pure-SQL trigram similarity with pg_trgm's exact padding because PGlite can't load the extension — the swap back is one migration when the real catalogue makes it slow. Proof: 121 rule scenes (118 old + 3 new) on PGlite and real Postgres, 19 race scenes unchanged. A second reader found 4 holes (the limit crowding out the exact part, one-size corrections wiping others, draft products leaking stock bands, loose numbers pulling parts in) — all fixed with scenes. Open: the real price list import waits for the shop.
