# Phase 1 core: the money and stock database

Part of `2026-11-01-power-store-plan.md`, steps 1.3, 1.4 and the database side of 1.6 and 1.7. Written 1 Nov 2026.

**Plan change.** The master plan said no code before the shop visit. The owner said "proceed" without naming a shop, so this builds only what is the same for any shop. It runs on the laptop: no accounts, spending or commits. The shop visit is still required before go-live, and can still change catalogue fields, limits and screens.

## Facts checked

- Gleey's ledger: three tables and a deferred trigger that refuses unbalanced transactions, `supabase/migrations/20261015000000_core.sql:277-317`.
- Gleey's tests: `sim/edge.mjs` on PGlite, `sim/testpg.mjs` embedded Postgres 17 for races. `sim/stubs.sql` stands in for Supabase's auth schema and API roles.
- Nazia's stock edits a number with no trail, and money is `integer` (master plan, section 10). Neither is copied.
- Postgres.js double-encodes `$n::jsonb` strings, so race tests write `$n::text::jsonb`.

## Assumptions

Percentages are my judgement, not measurements.

| Assumption | Chance | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Row locks hold when 20 sellers race for the last unit | 90% | Race test on real Postgres |
| Tracking exact stock value keeps the ledger's stock account equal to stock, to the kobo | 95% | Edge test on thirds, plus 90-day simulation |
| 10 KB per sale is right to within double | 60% | Measure on real Postgres |
| PGlite handles roles, row-level security and deferred triggers the same way | 85% | Security tests, repeated on real Postgres |

## If this fails a month from now

1. **Money and stock drift apart without anyone noticing.** `books_check()` compares them and can run nightly with an alert.
2. **A retry on a bad network records a sale twice.** Every write takes a client key, tested sequentially and under a race.
3. **A staff login reaches something it shouldn't.** All writes go through role-checked functions, and tests try the forbidden routes as each role.

## Not in this step

Screens, Next.js, Supabase projects, gateways, deliveries and trips, supplier orders and landed cost, returns and credit notes, serial numbers in stock counts, repricing by exchange rate.

## Done means

## Results, 1 Nov 2026

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Rule scenes on PGlite (`node edge.mjs`) | 92 of 92 |
| The same scenes on real Postgres (`node edge.mjs --real`) | 92 of 92 |
| Race scenes on real Postgres (`node race.mjs`) | 13 of 13, 0 deadlocks |
| 90 days at 30 sales a day | 2,555 sales, books agreed all 90 days, 6.6 KB per sale |
| 90 days at 100 sales a day | 8,376 sales, books agreed all 90 days, 5.8 KB per sale |
| `counter_sale` in the database, on this laptop | About 17 ms median, under 48 ms for 95% |

**Assumptions, checked:** row locks held under race (measured). Stock value stayed equal to the ledger to the kobo (measured). 10 KB per sale was within double (measured 5.8 to 6.6 KB). PGlite and real Postgres gave the same results across all 92 scenes.

**The tests alone were not enough.** The first version passed its 69 scenes and 12 races. A second reader, not told my reasoning, then proved 15 serious bugs by attacking it. All are fixed, with scenes in `sim/edge.mjs` and one new race. The worst:

- A counter with a 0% discount limit could give a ₦100,000 panel away free by overpricing another line on the same order.
- Delivering 0.1% of a unit was accepted, so a whole panel could leave at 0.1% of its price.
- Counter staff could read an item's exact cost from an error message, and a storekeeper could work it out from error differences in 63 calls.
- `transfer_stock` with no from-location made stock from nothing, and the books could then never close.
- An entry dated before a closed month changed that month's closing balance.
- A reused key returned another order's result as if the request had worked.
- A cash refund with no till counted the cash out twice.
- Two counts of one shelf could take the same loss twice, and a bad count could never be refused.

**Decisions made while fixing, each easy to reverse after the shop visit:**

- Storekeepers no longer see shelf numbers, so a count stays blind. They work from pick lists, capped at 20 units.
- Opening balances are owner-only, positive only, and stop once a month has closed.
- Counter staff may pay only fuel, other running costs or banking from the drawer, up to ₦50,000 at a time.
- Unpaid orders always get the short hold, however often staff renew it.

**Known gaps, not fixed:** see "Known gaps" in `.opencode/skills/power-database/SKILL.md`. **Not tested:** PostgREST's real error pass-through, a real Supabase project, real phones, real staff, real paper invoices.
